Summary: A practical guide on writing clear, persuasive design documents by structuring your thoughts rigorously, editing ruthlessly, and practicing consistently.

- **Definition & Goal:**  
  A design document is like a mathematical proof—it must persuade readers that the proposed design is optimal, and writing it helps clarify and refine your own thinking.

- **Organize Thoughtfully:**  
  Avoid "spaghetti design docs"—each sentence should flow logically from the previous one, so the reader never feels surprised. Strive for clarity that makes even complex solutions seem obvious.

- **Anticipate Reader Objections:**  
  Model your readers' mental state. Address objections before they arise and guide them to the conclusion that your design is sound.

- **Edit for Brevity:**  
  Treat readers' attention as scarce. On average, cut ~30% of your first draft without losing substance. Reviewing others' docs with a red pen builds editing muscles.

- **Practice Extensively:**  
  The author credits AWS’s writing culture—silent reading and margin markup in meetings—for helping improve doc quality over hundreds of iterations.

- **Concrete Tips:**  
  - *Short Paragraphs:* Structure your doc as a chain of bullet-like thoughts where each paragraph conveys a single idea easily summarized in one sentence.
  - *Use Appendices:* Move complex calculations or simulations to appendices. They should support, not clutter, the main narrative.
