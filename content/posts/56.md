Summary: A guide on the principles and practices for successfully shipping projects in a large tech company environment.

- **Shipping is Hard:** The default state of a project is failure to ship; it requires a dedicated effort to make it the top priority, often championed by a single individual with end-to-end understanding.
- **Shipping is a Social Construct:** A project is considered "shipped" when the company's leadership acknowledges it as such, not merely when code is deployed or features are available to users.
- **Maintain Trust with Leadership:** Since leadership lacks technical context, it's crucial to maintain their trust through confident and competent communication, providing clear estimates and updates.
- **Anticipate and Mitigate Problems:** A key role of a project lead is to anticipate technical, coordination, or legal issues, and have the deep understanding to address them or have fallback plans.
- **Deploy Early and Often:** Continuously deploying the project, even in rough stages using feature flags, helps to uncover unforeseen issues and build confidence with stakeholders.
- **Stay Flexible:** Project leads should delegate implementation tasks to free up their time to address unexpected problems, especially as the launch date approaches.
